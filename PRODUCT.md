# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

AIChef is for home cooks who are looking at ingredients they already have and need a practical answer to "what can I make with this?" The primary context is quick, often mobile use in or near the kitchen: take or upload one photo, confirm what was detected, add pantry staples or constraints, and get a usable recipe without browsing through long recipe content.

## Product Purpose

AIChef turns an ingredient photo into a recipe flow: detect visible ingredients, let the user correct the list, collect meal preferences, generate a coherent recipe, and show what must be bought or intentionally omitted. Success means the user trusts the app enough to cook from the result, understands why some ingredients were left out, and can quickly ask for a different recipe without starting over.

## Positioning

AIChef is the playful kitchen assistant that turns what is already on hand into a cookable plan, without recipe-blog clutter or generic AI-tool ceremony.

## Brand Personality

The product personality is playful and inventive, but grounded in useful cooking guidance. It should feel like a capable home-cooking companion: creative enough to suggest unexpected meals, direct enough to be used while cooking, and confident enough that the user does not need to second-guess every step.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI SaaS aesthetics: shiny gradients, abstract prompt-demo layouts, and interface choices that make the product feel like a model showcase instead of a kitchen tool. Avoid recipe blog clutter: long intros, buried actions, ad-like visual density, and decorative content before the recipe. Avoid childlike cooking-app tropes that make the product feel unserious. Avoid clinical utility patterns that turn cooking into a sterile form-filling exercise.

## Design Principles

1. Keep the task close to the kitchen: every screen should help the user move from ingredients to cooking with minimal detours.
2. Be playful only where it improves confidence or delight; core controls, states, and recipe content stay clear and predictable.
3. Make AI output inspectable: detected ingredients, omitted ingredients, shopping needs, and retries should explain what happened without sounding defensive.
4. Prefer fast correction over perfect automation: users should be able to fix ingredients, preferences, and recipe direction quickly.
5. Preserve mobile-first focus: the interface should work comfortably in one hand, under imperfect lighting, while the user is handling food.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for the web app. Maintain readable contrast for body text, labels, placeholders, and state text; provide visible keyboard focus states; avoid color-only state communication; keep Polish UI copy direct and understandable; and respect reduced-motion preferences for loading, screen transitions, and any future animation.
